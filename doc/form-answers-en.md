## The team
We are three students aged 19 to 21 in Mathematical Engineering and Modelling at UNSTIM, Abomey, Benin: Adéyinka Laurinda Adanlao (machine learning engineer in training), Prosper Affoukou (team lead, systems thinker and data scientist) and Abel Kpokouta (full stack developer and video maker). We designed VIBES ourselves for about three months.

## The problem
Many students here lose whole nights to scrolling, games, anime and manga. These passions are not the issue. The issue is that scrolling is designed to hold you, nothing as attractive waits outside, and few young people dare to say what they need. The result is a very connected generation that is also very lonely. The WHO reports that 1 in 6 people worldwide is affected by loneliness, and that 17 to 21% of people aged 13 to 29 feel lonely. We see four layers: attraction, the void, the silence, and the invisible (well-being tools exist, but students do not know them or do not see themselves in them).

## The people
University students at UNSTIM first, then other campuses in Benin. We prioritise students with tight budgets, long commutes, little reliable information, or who hesitate to join an activity alone: newcomers, students from the provinces, young women. Since 21 September we have surveyed students on campus: more than 100 have answered and the welcome is very positive. This is a first field check, not a representative sample.

## The idea
VIBES is a lightweight web app with three gestures. Discover: find young people who share your passions and join activities, clubs or a small Crew, so the first time is never alone. Equip: My Safe Place, a private space to note your mood and get small practical gestures, and Tips VIBES, short cards written by young Beninese people in their own words and reviewed for health content. Anchor: small Étincelles (Sparks) instead of pressure, with no streaks, no rankings and no guilt. VIBES is prevention, not care: it gives no diagnosis or score and points to a trusted person or service when it is too much.

## The value
A first step that is easier to take. Students see the real cost, distance and duration before they commit, find free activities first, join a small group instead of arriving alone, take a private moment without being judged, and know where to find help. Everything is designed for small data plans and older phones.

## The difference
Most apps are measured by the time you spend in them. VIBES is measured by the time you spend in it less. Our central indicator is lived hours: the real hours of matches, walks, rehearsals and creating that VIBES helped trigger, confirmed on the spot. Time in the app is only a warning signal. Content is written by a youth editorial committee, in students' own words and culture. We do not claim that no other solution exists; we will benchmark existing solutions in Benin and West Africa during the project.

## Access and safety
Light web app, cost and distance filters, French first (local languages being studied), beginner-friendly activities with accessibility information. Public activities only, student events checked before publication, Crew chat only for registered people with no private messages, minimum data collected, My Safe Place private, health content reviewed, and a "Need help now?" access on every screen.

## The plan
We build the MVP ourselves, with our own means. The award takes VIBES from one campus to a solid base for several universities. Proposed use of $5,000: wider surveys in other schools ($1,000), a youth-led awareness campaign ($1,000), a 4 to 6 week supervised pilot with campus ambassadors and data bundles for testers ($1,000), infrastructure such as domain, secure hosting and data protection ($750), supervision of health content by licensed professionals and ambassador training in listening ($750), and measurement plus an open learning report ($500). With a smaller award we keep the same split and scale the pilot down. The pilot asks: do students feel more connected after a few weeks, come back on their own, and take up new habits? No result is claimed yet. We will share an open learning report with the YTH community.
