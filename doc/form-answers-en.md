# VIBES · Answers for the online application (English)

## The team
We are three students at UNSTIM, Abomey, Benin: Charmel Prosper Affoukou (team lead, systems thinker and data scientist), Adéyinka Laurinda Adanlao (machine learning engineer in training and front-end developer) and Judicaël Karol Doboevi (business development and product marketing). We have designed VIBES ourselves for about three months and are building the first web MVP.

## The problem
Many students here lose whole nights to scrolling, games, anime and manga. These passions are not the issue. The issue is that scrolling is designed to hold you, nothing as attractive waits outside, and few young people dare to say what they need. The result is a very connected generation that is also very lonely. The WHO reports that 1 in 6 people worldwide is affected by loneliness, and that 17 to 21% of people aged 13 to 29 feel lonely. We see four layers: attraction, the void, the silence, and the invisible (well-being tools exist, but students do not know them or do not see themselves in them).

## The people
University students at UNSTIM first, then other campuses in Benin. We prioritise students with tight budgets, long commutes, little reliable information, or who hesitate to join an activity alone: newcomers, students from the provinces, young women. We ran a first short survey with 10 students from four schools. It is a signal, not proof (see "The difference" below for what it shows and what it does not).

## The idea
VIBES is a lightweight web app with three gestures. Discover: find young people who share your passions and join activities, clubs or a small Crew, so the first time is never alone. Equip: My Safe Place, a private space to note your mood and get small practical gestures, and Tips VIBES, short cards written by young Beninese people in their own words and reviewed for health content. Anchor: small Étincelles (Sparks) instead of pressure, with no streaks, no rankings and no guilt. VIBES is prevention, not care: it gives no diagnosis or score and points to a trusted person or service when it is too much.

## What young people told us (first survey, n = 10)
Seven of ten spend more than 6 hours a day on their phone in free time, and five say it is too long for them. All ten put social media or videos in their top three free-time activities. The main barriers to offline activities were having no one to go with (4), distance (4), shyness (3) and lack of time (3). Nobody chose budget or "I don't know what exists" as a main barrier. After a short description, 7 said they were interested in trying VIBES, 3 said maybe, none said no. Seven would spend up to 1,000 FCFA on a group activity. This is a small convenience sample, so we read it as a first signal and plan a larger survey. (Do not quote free-text answers: nobody gave permission.)

## The value
A first step that is easier to take. Students see the real cost, distance and duration before they commit, find free activities first, join a small group instead of arriving alone, take a private moment without being judged, and know where to find help. Everything is designed for small data plans and older phones.

## The difference
Most apps are measured by the time you spend in them. VIBES is measured by the time you spend in it less. Our central indicator is lived hours: the real hours of matches, walks, rehearsals and creating that VIBES helped trigger, confirmed on the spot. Time in the app is only a warning signal. Content is planned to be written with young people, in students' own words and culture. The closest product we found is ChillChezCharlee in France, which groups nearby students around weekend wishes and has won a French national award; VIBES differs by adding a private well-being space and tips by young Beninese, a Benin context (light web app, cost and distance first), and lived hours as the success measure. Lynk is online one-to-one video, a different idea. We have only reviewed their public websites.

## Access and safety
Light web app, cost and distance filters, French first (local languages being studied), beginner-friendly activities with accessibility information. Planned safeguards: public activities only, student events checked before publication, Crew chat only for registered people with no private messages, minimum data collected, My Safe Place private, health content reviewed by professionals, and a "Need help now?" access on every screen.

## The plan
We build the MVP ourselves, with our own means. Roadmap (targets, in months): Nov–Dec 2026 listen (survey to 30+ students, mockup tests); Jan–Mar 2027 build the web MVP with 20 to 30 campus testers; Apr–Jun 2027 prepare (wider surveys, ambassadors, health review, hosting); Jul–Sep 2027 run a 4 to 6 week supervised pilot outside exam periods; Oct–Dec 2027 publish the open learning report and decide on a second campus. The first two phases are self-funded; the others start when funds arrive. The award takes VIBES from one campus to a solid base for several universities. Proposed use of $5,000: wider surveys in other schools ($1,000), a youth-led awareness campaign ($1,000), a 4 to 6 week supervised pilot with campus ambassadors and data bundles for testers ($1,000), infrastructure such as domain, secure hosting and data protection ($750), supervision of health content by licensed professionals and ambassador training in listening ($750), and measurement plus an open learning report ($500). With a smaller award we keep the same split and scale the pilot down. The pilot asks: do students feel more connected after a few weeks, come back on their own, and take up new habits? No result is claimed yet. We will share an open learning report with the YTH community.
